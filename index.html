About the data

Levels

The four achievement levels are fixed cut scores on the Smarter Balanced scale. For grade 11 math: Level 1 below 2543, Level 2 from 2543, Level 3 ("standard met") from 2628, Level 4 from 2718. "Met" means Level 3 or 4. Each grade and subject has its own cut scores. First score of Levels 2, 3 and 4, math: grade 3: 2381, 2436, 2501; grade 4: 2411, 2485, 2549; grade 5: 2455, 2528, 2579; grade 6: 2473, 2552, 2610; grade 7: 2484, 2567, 2635; grade 8: 2504, 2586, 2653; grade 11: 2543, 2628, 2718. English language arts: grade 3: 2367, 2432, 2490; grade 4: 2416, 2473, 2533; grade 5: 2442, 2502, 2582; grade 6: 2457, 2531, 2618; grade 7: 2479, 2552, 2649; grade 8: 2487, 2567, 2668; grade 11: 2493, 2583, 2682.

In 2026 the state renamed the levels (Minimal, Developing, Proficient, Advanced) without changing the cut scores. 2026 results are due in October 2026 and can be added directly.

Missing and unusual years

No test was given in 2020. In 2021 fewer than half of 11th graders tested, and about a fifth of students in grades 3 to 8; those who tested scored higher than other years' students. 2021 is drawn hatched and should not be read as part of a trend.

Statewide figure

The statewide figure can show either subject (math or English language arts) and any tested grade (3 to 8 and 11). The default is grade 11 math. The "follow a cohort" view strings together the statewide result for grade 3 in one year, grade 4 the next year, and so on through grade 8, then grade 11 three years later (grades 9 and 10 are not tested). Cohorts are listed when at least two of their grades were tested. This is a synthetic cohort: each bar is everyone tested in that grade and year, not the same students followed over time. Students move into and out of California, repeat or skip a grade, and are absent or exempted at different rates, and the grade 11 cohorts of 2022 and 2023 were counted as economically disadvantaged on a changed basis (see "Student groups"). Each grade has its own cut scores, so the levels say whether students met the standard for that grade, not where they sit on one scale. Every cohort that was in a tested grade in 2020 has a gap there, and a cohort tested in 2021 has a hatched bar; the note under the chart gives the share of enrolled students who tested that year.

School results

The state does not publish results for a group of fewer than 11 testers at a school. Pooled results (the grouped-school chart) therefore leave out students in small groups. For all students this is under 1% of testers after 2016 (3% in 2015). For smaller groups it is larger: in 2019 the school rows cover 82% of Black, 77% of Filipino, 73% of multiracial, and 89% of English-learner 11th graders statewide, against 99% of Hispanic and 96% of White 11th graders.

The three school figures show grade 11 math only; the school file holds no other grade or subject. When schools are pooled, each school counts in proportion to its number of tested students. The pooled all-student results match the statewide figures within 0.4 percentage points in every year.

The one-school figure offers every student group the state reports for that school (28 groups, when all have published results) and can draw two groups side by side, one pair of bars per year. From the first year a school appears in the state file, every year without a published result for the chosen group is marked "Not reported": the state suppresses any result for fewer than 11 students, and the 2017, 2018, 2024 and 2025 files leave such rows out altogether, so a small school can be missing from a year entirely. It is a gap, not a zero; hovering it says whether the group's row was suppressed, absent, or the school had no row that year.

The number of schools with published results changes from year to year as schools open, close and cross the 11-tester line. The "same schools both years" option removes this turnover. Between 2019 and 2025 it matters: schools that dropped out after 2019 had a median of 4% met, while schools new by 2025 had a median of 21%.

School characteristics

Unduplicated pupil percentage (UPP): the share of a school's students who are low-income (eligible for free or reduced-price meals), English learners, or foster youth, each student counted once. It is the state's school-funding measure. Here it is the school's average for 2016–17 through 2019–20. It is not a poverty rate.

Census-tract measures describe the neighborhood where the school is located, not its students: median household income, the share of residents below the federal poverty line, and the share of adults 25 and older with a bachelor's degree. American Community Survey 2015–2019 five-year estimates, in nominal dollars.

A–G completion: the share of each four-year graduating cohort that finished the course sequence required for UC and CSU admission, pooled over the classes of 2017–2019. School-years with known reporting errors in the state file are left out.

District groupings use the district recorded in the state's school directory, so they include charter schools the district authorized. With charter schools left out, the pooled results for San Diego Unified and Fresno Unified match the state's own district totals within 0.2 points; Los Angeles Unified's district totals also include its affiliated charters.

Thirds, quarters and fifths are cut so each holds about the same number of tested students, not the same number of schools. Schools missing a characteristic are left out of that grouping.

Within-school gap: the share of students at Level 3 or 4 among not socioeconomically disadvantaged students minus the share among socioeconomically disadvantaged students at the same school, in percentage points, pooled over the years in which the school reports both groups. Schools that never report both groups are left out.

Regions: Bay Area (Alameda, Contra Costa, Marin, Napa, San Francisco, San Mateo, Santa Clara, Solano, Sonoma); Sacramento area (Sacramento, Placer, El Dorado, Yolo, Sutter, Yuba); Central Coast (Monterey, San Benito, San Luis Obispo, Santa Barbara, Santa Cruz, Ventura); San Joaquin Valley (San Joaquin, Stanislaus, Merced, Madera, Fresno, Kings, Tulare, Kern); Inland Empire (Riverside, San Bernardino); San Diego and Imperial; Los Angeles County; Orange County; North State (the 16 counties north of the Sacramento area); Sierra and Eastern (Alpine, Amador, Calaveras, Inyo, Mariposa, Mono, Tuolumne).

Student groups

Groups are as defined by the state. "Economically disadvantaged" means eligible for free or reduced-price meals or neither parent a high school graduate. The number of economically disadvantaged 11th graders rose from 244,000 in 2022 to 269,000 in 2023 while total testers barely changed, so that group's trend across 2022–2023 partly reflects a change in who is counted. From 2022, some students are reported as neither male nor female, so the two gender groups do not always add to the total.

Sources

California Department of Education: CAASPP Smarter Balanced research files 2015–2025 (statewide and school rows), public schools directory, LCFF unduplicated pupil counts, adjusted cohort graduation files (A–G). U.S. Census Bureau: American Community Survey. Built October 2026.
